Strengths

These are what to protect, and what to lead with when talking to the club.

1

A plus changeup that plays to both sides

42.5% whiffs per swing, 40% chase, .191 xwOBA, 29.7% strikeout rate, and no barrels. Same-side hitters, who rarely see much of a righty's changeup, whiff on 52% of their swings against it. Scouts called it plus in high school, and it still is.

138 pitches · vs RHH 56 pitches, 52% whiff/swing · vs LHH 82 pitches, 37.5% whiff/swing, .190 xwOBA
2

A big-league fastball

96.9 mph average, 99.2 max, with 13.2 inches of ride and a flat approach angle (−4.4°), a shape that plays at the top of the zone. .269 xwOBA, 3.1% barrels, and no home runs allowed on it.

216 pitches · velocity by month: 97.4 (6 pitches), 97.0, 96.9, 97.3, 96.8, 96.6. Velo held after the IL stint.
3

He finishes hitters once he's ahead

With two strikes: 38.8% strikeouts and 43.1% chase. In pitcher's counts (0-1, 0-2, 1-2, 2-2) hitters produce a .185 xwOBA. When he's ahead, the stuff wins.

187 two-strike pitches · 98 batters reached a pitcher's count
4

He limits hard contact

4.5% barrels and 35.7% hard-hit on batted balls, both better than league average. Overall xwOBA .307 is about league level even with the walks. With the bases empty: .265 xwOBA and a 6.3% walk rate. His 3.98 xERA and 4.16 FIP back it up: the 5.35 ERA overstates the damage.

112 batted balls · 79 batters with the bases empty · .319 wOBA vs .307 xwOBA

Areas for improvement

Ordered by how much each one costs him.

1

Walks, and the counts that cause them

A 13.0% walk rate: 23 walks, two of them intentional, in 38.2 innings. In hitter's counts, 34% of batters walked. Of the 30 non-intentional plate appearances that reached three balls, 21 ended in walks. He threw a first-pitch strike 57% of the time, and no pitch stood out: 55–59% for every pitch type. Strike one comes down to command, whatever he throws.

Walk rate by month: Mar 9% · Apr 16% · May 3% · Jun 22% (then the injured list) · Aug 12% · Sep 12%. May is the version of him that pounds the zone.
2

The sinker gets hit, and it's what he leans on when behind

.475 xwOBA, .574 wOBA, 54.8% hard-hit, 10.8% whiffs per swing, 1 strikeout in 141 pitches. It allowed 15 hits, 2 of his 3 home runs, and 9 walks. He throws it on the first pitch (46 times) and on 31% of pitches when behind. Its spin axis almost matches the four-seam's (227° vs 228°), so it looks like a four-seamer with five inches less ride, which hitters square up.

vs RHH .501 xwOBA (69 pitches) · vs LHH .450 (72 pitches). Both sides hit it.
3

Slider shape: a weapon against righties, one to locate better against lefties

Against righties it works: .240 xwOBA, 40.5% whiff per swing, 9 strikeouts in 22 plate appearances. Against lefties, the target is better location: they chased it 14.6% of the time, and when it caught the zone they did damage (.351 xwOBA, 61.5% hard-hit). Overall chase of 21.6% is low for a slider. The shape is a short, gyro-style 88 mph pitch with almost no sweep (−2.7 in).

vs LHH 92 pitches but only 13 batted balls. The trend is clear; the sample is small.
4

Traffic makes it worse

With men on base, his walk rate climbs to 18.4%, and to 23.3% with runners in scoring position. Contact quality slips too: .344 xwOBA with men on and .375 with runners in scoring position, against .265 with the bases empty. High-leverage relievers get paid to strand runners. His delivery out of the stretch, tempo and pitch calling with men on are all worth a look.

98 batters with men on · 73 with runners in scoring position (the 2 intentional walks fall here)
5

Short extension for a 6'4" pitcher

5.8 feet of extension, shorter than typical for his size, so his 96.9 plays like 96.4 by Statcast's perceived velocity. That's a lower-half and delivery question, and the force-plate mound and MoCap will show where it's coming from.

Changeup extension is longer (6.2 ft) than the fastball's (5.8 ft), worth checking on the mound
6

Staying on the field

Right shoulder tendinitis in 2026 (June 21 – Aug 19); in 2025, a Triple-A injured-list stint and a batted ball off the elbow on Aug 23 (day-to-day, back Aug 29); and Tommy John surgery in 2021. Games and innings feed every arbitration comparison, so availability is the off-season plan's first job.

Right-handed hitters' .389 wOBA against him is mostly batted-ball luck. His xwOBA against righties (.318) is close to lefties' (.301), and most of the damage traces to the sinker. Also seen on small samples: road walk rate 18.8% vs 7.6% at home (85 and 92 batters), and a rough 8th inning (.443 wOBA, .369 xwOBA, 62 batters).

Quick wins

Pitch-mix and game-plan adjustments he can make in spring training without touching the delivery. Worth bringing to the Rockies' pitching staff with these numbers.

1

Cut the sinker in half, starting with righties and when behind

Replace most of those sinkers with the four-seam: .269 xwOBA against .475, and 21.9% hard-hit against 54.8%. Strikes wouldn't suffer, since both pitches draw first-pitch strikes at about the same rate (57% and 59%). Keep a few so hitters still have to respect it.

About 70 fewer sinkers over a season like 2026, the pitch behind 2 of his 3 homers and 9 of his 23 walks
2

Throw more changeups to right-handed hitters

52% whiffs per swing against righties, yet only 18% of his pitches to them, against 23% to lefties. It's his best pitch; he should lean on it more.

Target: roughly 25% to righties · 56 pitches and 12 plate appearances so far, so test it in spring
3

Match the secondary pitch to the hitter

Lead with the slider against righties (.240 xwOBA) and the changeup against lefties (.190 xwOBA, 42.6% chase). Against lefties, the slider comes second until he locates it better (.351 xwOBA, 14.6% chase).

Small batted-ball samples on each side (12–18). Start there and re-check with spring data.
4

Have a pitch he trusts in three-ball counts

21 of 30 non-intentional three-ball plate appearances ended in walks. Give him a set plan: four-seam in the zone, above the belt. It's the pitch that allowed no home runs.

How performance has turned into arbitration money

Under the current CBA, arbitrators weigh the player's contribution in the platform year (the season just before), the length and consistency of his career contribution, his past pay, what comparable players with similar service earn, physical condition, and recent team performance.

1 · Role

Traditional counting statistics, particularly saves for relievers, have historically carried significant weight in arbitration comparisons. Holds count too, usually for less.

2 · Volume: games and innings

Counting stats need innings. Injured-list time shrinks every one of them, which makes availability an arbitration asset as well as a baseball one.

3 · Results: ERA, strikeouts

Comparisons have historically leaned on traditional stats. Advanced numbers like xwOBA can support the case but carry less established weight. For Jaden, ERA is where the walks show up.

Raises are negotiated off the prior salary, so the first arbitration number becomes the base for the years after it. Under today's rules, that would make the 2028 season his most valuable platform.

Player Communication and Questions

Lead with

  • The changeup and the fastball are big-league weapons, and hitters rarely barrel him (4.5%).
  • The club trusted him in big spots: a 1.55 average leverage index. His 3.98 xERA and 4.16 FIP say he pitched better than his 5.35 ERA.
  • May 2026 (1.93 ERA, ~3% walks, ~31% strikeouts) is the version of him that pounds the zone.
  • Three pitch-mix changes that ask nothing extra of his arm: fewer sinkers, more changeups to righties, and the right secondary pitch for each side.

Ask him

  • Does he trust the sinker, or is it what he throws when he's unsure?
  • What's different for him from the stretch?
  • How does the slider feel against lefties? Does he want more sweep?
  • Did the shoulder change how anything felt in June?
  • What role does he want in 2028?
